## Thesis
Book review (Nov 1992 JAE 46/2) of William J. Mitchell's "The Logic of Architecture: Design, Computation, and Cognition" (MIT Press, 1990). Mitchell attempts to formalize architectural design reasoning using first-order predicate calculus and state-space search — turning design into computation.

## Key claims (from review)
- Architecture as "an art of separation and distinction-making."
- Formal predicate calculus can describe design objects and relations.
- Design becomes state-space search under axioms (like classical CAD).
- Reasoning about designs requires a critical language grounded in model-theoretic semantics.

## Relevance to defi-investor
**Tangential.** The intellectual link is: formal reasoning about design choices. Our pre-registration architecture (Decision 4) is doing something structurally similar — reducing hypothesis design to a formalized specification that can be automatically evaluated. Not load-bearing but confirms that formal-reasoning frameworks in other domains use similar structure.
