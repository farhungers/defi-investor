## Thesis
Writing is the medium of thinking, not what follows research. Niklas Luhmann's slip-box method — atomic notes, linked non-hierarchically, no rigid categories — enables insight to compound over years. The organization layer stays simple; complexity lives in content and connections.

## Key claims
- **Writing is the only thing that matters.** Ideas that aren't written don't exist as thinking tools.
- **Simplicity is paramount.** Rigid hierarchies fossilize prematurely and hide the connections that generate insight.
- **Nobody starts from scratch.** Existing notes generate new ideas; the slip-box becomes a dialogue partner.
- **Let the work carry you forward.** Structure enables flow; you follow what's interesting, not what's planned.
- **Three note types**: fleeting (temporary), literature (from sources), permanent (your synthesis, atomic, linkable).
- **Luhmann's productivity**: 58 books + hundreds of articles over 30 years, all attributed to the slip-box.

## Relevance to defi-investor
- **The library architecture in this repo IS the Zettelkasten pattern.** `docs/library/LIB_NNNN` = literature notes; `docs/library/permanent/` = permanent notes; `docs/library/MOC.md` = map of content (not a folder hierarchy).
- **JIT reading protocol** — Ahrens's insight that "reading with a question in mind produces insight; reading without one produces passive consumption" justifies our decision-first, read-on-demand cadence.
- **Second Law compatibility** — the slip-box grows without forcing premature conclusions; matches de Prado's discipline of not iterating on preliminary results.

## Open questions
- Do we need permanent-note discipline from day one, or is it OK to have only literature notes until specific themes reach critical mass?
- How much of the slip-box's power relies on daily practice? Our cadence is bursty, not daily.
