## Thesis
Being good at science and engineering is a learnable skill, not innate talent. Hamming, based on his Bell Labs career, gives concrete practices: work on important problems, develop tools, communicate rigorously, treat mistakes as data.

## Key claims (from reputation)
- "You get what you measure." Choose what to optimize carefully.
- "What are the most important problems in your field?" is the question few ask.
- Communication multiplies impact; do the research AND the writeup.
- Style of thought matters as much as content.

## Relevance to defi-investor
Background discipline. Reinforces the Zettelkasten (LIB_0005) practice — writing IS thinking. Also relevant to how we phrase problems: are we asking the most important question, or just an available one?

## Open questions
- Are we working on an important-enough problem, or a tractable-adjacent one? Worth periodic re-examination.
